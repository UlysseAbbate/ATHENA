Privacy and acceptable use

Athena is a private working site (LOVELACE, LUCIEN and LIBBY). It is not open to the public and is not indexed by search engines. This page says what the site keeps about the people who use it and the people recorded in it, and what is expected of anyone given access.

What the site keeps about you

  • Your account: your email address, the name you give, your password (stored only as a one-way hash by the sign-in service, never readable), the access you have been given, and when you last signed in.
  • What you add or change: entries carry the name or email of the person who made them. In LOVELACE an activity log records who added, changed or deleted which entry and when (not the contents).
  • Failed sign-ins with an access code: a scrambled fingerprint of the connection (never the address itself), deleted after a day, to slow down guessing.

The site uses one cookie and your browser's storage to keep you signed in and to remember your light or dark setting. There are no advertising or tracking cookies and no analytics.

People recorded in the site

The programs hold working records about other people: for example contacts, stakeholders and volunteers (names, organisations, contact details and notes) in LOVELACE, and in LIBBY the names of parliamentarians and staff against roles, rotas, availability, room bookings and notes on parliamentary business. Much of LIBBY and LUCIEN is public information from Parliament and published sources.

These records are kept for the work of the team and are seen only by people who have been given access to that program at the right level. Because the site supports political work, a record can imply someone's political opinions; such records are treated with particular care.

Where it is held, and who else handles it

  • Supabase holds the database and sign-in.
  • Cloudflare serves the site.
  • GitHub holds the site's code and runs its scheduled updates (public sources only).
  • Anthropic (Claude) is used when you press a button that asks for a draft, a summary or an answer (for example Parliamentary process, a Bill update or a suggested question). The text you send and the public passages it is answered from go to Anthropic for that purpose. Do not put personal or confidential details into those boxes.

How long things are kept

Records are kept while they are needed for the team's work and are deleted when they are not. Accounts are removed when a person no longer needs access. Entries can be deleted by the people with the right level; an administrator can delete an account and everything tied to it.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask to see what is held about you, to have it corrected or deleted, and to object to how it is used. Ask the person who gave you access, or the site's administrator, who will answer within a month. If you are not satisfied you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk).

Acceptable use

  • Your account is for you alone. Do not share your password; shared access codes are only for the group they were issued to.
  • Use the site only for the work it is for, and only look at what you need.
  • Do not copy records about people out of the site unless your work needs it, and keep any copy as safe as the site keeps it.
  • Tell the administrator at once if you think an account, a code or any data has been seen by someone who should not have seen it.
  • Answers and drafts written by Claude are a starting point: check them against the sources shown before relying on them.
